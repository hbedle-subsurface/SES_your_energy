## The short version

Six questions. Seven possible results. Zero energy knowledge required.

This is a pure personality quiz. The questions never mention an energy source by name. Students answer based on how they actually think and move through the world -- and then land on a result that connects those instincts to something happening in the energy sector right now. The education happens in the result card, not the questions. The goal is curiosity, not comprehension.

---

## Why this exists

Most people have no idea how interesting the energy sector is. They know about solar panels and gas prices. They don't know that there's heat sitting under their feet that has been there for 4 billion years and we're only now figuring out how to use it. They don't know the US electric grid was designed in the 1960s and is being reinvented from scratch. They don't know Oklahoma generates over 40% of its electricity from wind and ranks second in the country for wind resources.

This quiz is the entry point for that conversation. It doesn't lecture. It reflects something true about the student back at them -- and then shows them where that instinct lives in the energy world. A student who sees themselves as the person who fixes what's already broken lands on Natural Gas + Carbon Capture and reads about why managing the existing energy system intelligently is one of the most consequential roles in the transition. A student who's drawn to things nobody's figured out yet lands on Wave/Tidal and reads about an open field that's barely been touched.

The quiz is a sibling to the SES Role quiz ("What Kind of Energy Person Are You?") which connects personality to career. This one comes before that -- it's the hook that makes someone curious enough to ask the career question at all.

## The seven results

- **Solar** -- high output, full presence, needs to recharge after; the student who shows up completely and then disappears; connects to the most-installed energy source in the world and its rapidly expanding frontier (agrivoltaics, solar paint, floating arrays)
- **Wind** -- always moving, most productive when unconstrained; connects to Oklahoma's own story and the fastest-growing electricity source on the planet
- **Geothermal** -- slow, deep, reliable, underestimated; connects to a field that is quietly about to stop being early-stage
- **Nuclear** -- dense, intense, a lot going on, controversial before people do the reading; connects to small modular reactors, nuclear-powered data centers, and one of the most genuinely open debates in energy
- **You're a Battery** -- the coordinator, the systems thinker, the one who makes everything else work; connects to grid-scale storage as the central unsolved engineering problem of the next 20 years
- **Wave/Tidal** -- patient, drawn to overlooked things, operating on a long timeline; connects to an almost completely untapped resource covering 71% of the planet
- **Natural Gas + Carbon Capture** -- pragmatic, iterative, fixes what exists; connects to the honest reality that 40% of US electricity is still natural gas and the transition requires someone managing that intelligently

Each result includes a personality read that connects the student's instincts to the energy source, a wild fact designed to genuinely surprise them, and a brief note about the SES Certificate and how it connects to this area.

## The design intent

The questions are deliberately opaque. No option names an energy source. No option assumes technical knowledge. A student who has never thought about energy for a single second can answer every question based purely on personality and still land somewhere meaningful.

This is intentional. The moment an option says "Nuclear -- high energy density, zero emissions" it becomes a knowledge test and students who don't recognize the vocabulary opt out. The moment it becomes a knowledge test, the undeclared sophomore who would have been a great nuclear policy researcher picks something else because they didn't know the right answer.

The question writing principle: if a student could answer it honestly without knowing anything about energy, it's a good question. If knowing about energy would change their answer, it's too transparent.

## The curiosity hook

Every result card opens with a "wild fact" -- one concrete, shareable thing about that energy source that most people genuinely don't know:

- Solar: more energy hits the Earth in one hour than the world uses in a year
- Wind: turbines being built today are taller than the Eiffel Tower
- Geothermal: the center of the Earth is roughly as hot as the surface of the sun
- Nuclear: a golf-ball-sized piece of uranium contains as much energy as 1,500 tons of coal
- Battery: the US electric grid has almost no large-scale storage -- electricity is used the moment it's made
- Wave/Tidal: tidal energy is completely predictable; we know when the tides will rise and fall for the next thousand years
- Natural Gas + CCS: natural gas currently generates about 40% of US electricity and isn't going away overnight

These facts are the thing students screenshot. They're not there to teach -- they're there to make someone want to keep reading.

## Who it's for

Any OU undergraduate who has never thought about energy. Designed especially for students who would not self-select into an energy quiz -- which is most of them.

Useful for:

- Poster and flyer QR codes across campus ("find out what kind of energy you are")
- Social media link-in-bio
- Tabling events where you need something for someone to do while they're standing there
- A low-stakes first touchpoint before directing students to the SES Role quiz
- Classroom visits in any department -- the questions work for everyone

---

## Relationship to the SES Role quiz

These two quizzes are designed to work together as a funnel:

**What's Your Energy?** (this quiz) -- personality-first, energy-type result, plants curiosity, no career content  
**What Kind of Energy Person Are You?** -- instinct-first, career-role result, explains how a student's major connects to the SES Certificate

A student might take this one from a poster QR code, get curious, then take the role quiz and find out there's actually a certificate program at their own university that fits exactly what they're studying. The email CTA on both quizzes points to the same person (Nova at SES@ou.edu) and uses the same low-stakes framing: one conversation, no commitment.
